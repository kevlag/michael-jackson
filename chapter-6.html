In 1991, Michael released his eighth solo album Dangerous, his first without Quincy Jones in 16 years. The album marked a different direction for Michael and mixed various genres.

Including R&B, funk, gospel, hip-hop, rock, industrial, and classical. It included the hit single "Black or White", with an accompanying music video directed by Landis and featuring a cameo appearance by child star Macaulay Culkin. The final minutes of the video featured Michael making sexual gesturing and violently damaging cars and buildings, which drew criticism from some viewers. Michael issued an apology and edited the video to remove these elements.

Michael's music continued to enjoy widespread popularity in the following years. In 1993, he performed at several important events, including the halftime show of Superbowl XXVII. However, 1993 also saw the first of several child molestation allegations against Michael, when a 13-year-old boy claimed that the music star had fondled him. Michael was known to have sleepovers with boys at his Neverland Ranch, but this was the first public charge of wrongdoing. The police searched the ranch, but said they found no evidence to support the claim. The following year, Michael settled the case out of court with the boy's family.

In the 2003 television documentary Living with Michael Jackson, British journalist Martin Bashir spent several months with the singer, even getting him to discuss his relationships with children. Michael admitted that he continued to have children sleep over at his ranch, even after the 1993 allegations and that sometimes he slept with the children in his bed. "Why can't you share your bed? That's the most loving thing to do, to share your bed with someone," Michael told Bashir.
